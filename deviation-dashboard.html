What the data shows

Five findings a Quality Manager could act on this quarter.

Closure time improved 35% over the period — from 30.0 days to 19.5 days

Average closure in the first six months was 30.0 days against 19.5 days in the final six. The improvement is steady rather than stepped, which points to a process change embedding over time rather than a one-off backlog clearance.

Documentation errors are the single largest root cause at 23.8% of deviations

57 of 240 deviations trace to documentation error, ahead of procedure-not-followed (49) and equipment failure (36). Combined, documentation and procedural failures account for 44% of the total — both are human-factor causes addressable through SOP clarity and training rather than capital spend.

CAPA reduces recurrence by roughly two thirds

Deviations with a CAPA raised recurred at 4.6%, against 12.4% for those closed without one. This is the clearest argument in the dataset for holding the line on CAPA even when a deviation looks minor and the pressure is to close it quickly.

Manufacturing carries the highest deviation load, but Warehouse & Supply Chain is close behind

Manufacturing accounts for 70 deviations and Warehouse & Supply Chain 61. The warehouse figure is worth attention — supply chain deviations are often under-reported relative to production floor issues, so a near-parity count may indicate either genuinely elevated risk or a recent improvement in reporting culture.

39 deviations closed past their severity-based target

Overdue closures concentrate in the earlier months and thin out considerably by 2026, consistent with the overall closure-time improvement. Residual overdue cases skew Major and Critical, where investigation complexity is genuinely higher.

Method & data provenance Synthetic dataset

This dashboard runs on a synthetic dataset of 240 deviation records generated to mirror the structure, severity distribution, and closure patterns of a real GMP quality system. No confidential or employer data is used — this is deliberate, and the same discipline I would apply to any data leaving a regulated site.

The generator (generate_data.py) models a realistic severity mix, department distribution, and a closure-time improvement trend across the period. Recurrence probability is conditioned on whether a CAPA was raised. Aggregation and analysis are in Python; the front end is Chart.js with no framework dependency.

Why this project: deviation and CAPA performance is the metric set I worked with directly as a Quality Associate, where I reduced deviation resolution time by 30% through structured root cause investigation. This dashboard is the reporting layer I would build to make that kind of improvement visible to management month by month.
